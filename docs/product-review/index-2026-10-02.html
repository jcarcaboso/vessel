A trader's review of Vessel: summary

This page summarises the review documents in this folder: the trader review, the UI/UX audit, the roadmap and the competitor benchmark. Examples use a made-up 10,000 USDC Hyperliquid account. Fees use Hyperliquid tier 0 (0.045% taker, 0.015% maker) and funding uses the baseline rate (0.00125%/h). All numbers were recomputed by script. This is a proposal, not investment advice and not an authorisation to build.

Verdict

Right philosophy, strong foundations, but the loop is not closed. Vessel separates plan, fill, price observation and retrospective review better than any journal benchmarked. It already has live Hyperliquid candles, drawing tools, and evidence capture with markup. But a trader cannot complete one cycle of plan → execute → review → learn:

  • Plays cannot be saved. A reload deletes the thesis, levels, drawings and captures.
  • No money figures. Risk in $, reward-to-risk, margin, fees, funding and liquidation show "Not calculated" or are absent.
  • Imported fills cannot be grouped into trades or linked to a plan, and every row says "Unassigned".
  • Nothing measures results in R (multiples of planned risk), so nothing can be learned from history.

M1

Stop scope (position or per entry), break-even/trailing rules, time stop, and non-price invalidation. If a rule isn't written down, the review can't check it.

Issues & bugs ui-ux-audit.md §7 and the code inventory

Product gaps today

  • Draft only lives in memory. The backend Play has only account, contract and status, and there are no Play CRUD routes.
  • Evidence storage exists on the server, but nothing can create the parent Play, so images never upload.
  • The sizing assistant is a "Deferred" placeholder, although the approved prototype had working maths.
  • Five free-text journal tabs and nothing structured, so nothing can be analysed.
  • Import: latest 2,000 fills, manual refresh only, Activity capped at 100 rows. Funding payments are not imported.
  • No strategies, lifecycle, alerts, tags or analytics. Status is a fixed "Draft" badge.
  • Manual accounts can't update their value after creation and get no chart.

Where Vessel can win

  • Plan first, with revisions. None of the journals reviewed keeps a versioned multi-entry plan with a frozen snapshot.
  • Perps-native maths. None of them shows liquidation versus stop on every fill path before the trade.
  • Concurrent Plays inside one position, linked per order. None of them handles this.
  • Honest uncertainty: n, ±SE, null not zero, observation not fill.
  • Private where it matters. Hyperliquid fills are public; your thesis and review stay on your own server.
  • Process over outcome, without a coach persona or gamified scores.
  • Free and self-hosted, against $197–$1,188 a year for paid journals.

Deliberately not copied

  • Backtesting, single "health" scores, social leaderboards, whale tracking.
  • AI that writes the thesis or the review: it breaks the "never fabricate" principle.

Nice to have

  • Risk-of-ruin and streak simulation · trade replay · Telegram/Discord · weekly review page
  • Opt-in AI over your own journal · daily-R calendar · Lighter · themes · mobile quick capture
  • Command palette (N new Play, L/S, E, C) · HIP-3, sub-accounts and vaults

Principles, risks and caveats

Principles every proposal keeps

  • A price touch or candle is an observation, never a fill.
  • No fabricated thesis, stop or plan for imported history. Retrospective inputs are labelled.
  • Concurrent Plays per account and instrument; links by order or fill, never guessed.
  • Read-only venue access. Vessel records and flags; it never blocks trading or places orders.
  • Unknown stays null. Exact decimal strings are kept.
  • Locked section order: capital above, chart left, journal below the chart, position right, summary below.

Risks to the concept

  • Friction kills journals: logging should take about a minute at most.
  • Over-cautious wording ("Not calculated" everywhere) reads as "can't help me".
  • Learning from noise: always show n and uncertainty.

How this was produced

  • Round 1: docs, a code inventory, a hands-on UI walkthrough on an isolated stack with live public Hyperliquid data, first draft.
  • Round 2: a critic playing a professional perps trader corrected fees, the liquidation formula and examples, and re-prioritised. HIP-3 claims were checked against the docs and only partly held.
  • Round 3: roadmap; a reviewer checked every number, link and 20 code references (fix: +1.83R, not +1.90R).
  • Round 4: competitor benchmark done. The accessibility audit, technical specs and the other mockups stopped at the session limit and are not done.

Not verified

  • Human timing, touch, screen readers, populated fill tables, venue errors, widths other than 1440 / 390.
  • TraderSync and HyperTracker details (third-party only). Fees and funding follow Hyperliquid docs at the time of writing.
